## Stochastic simulation (`project/stochastic/`)
- [ ] `lambda_risk` (Q-measure market price of risk) is deliberately left at its zero default rather than calibrated. Two blockers, documented in `hjm_model.py::_evolve_pcs`: (1) this project only has spot Treasury yields, not derivative prices, so there's no clean data to calibrate a real price of risk against without building an empirical term-premium estimator (e.g. Fama-Bliss-style excess-return regressions); (2) it's unclear whether `lambda_risk` is even a free parameter in this construction, or redundant with the `_hjm_drift` no-arbitrage adjustment `simulate()` already applies directly to the forward curve under Q -- that relationship hasn't been derived. Needs a methodology decision before any calibration is trustworthy.
- [ ] `HJMModel.simulate_with_parameter_uncertainty` isn't wired into the walk-forward backtest (`project/stochastic/backtest.py`) -- each origin refits a plug-in point-estimate OU only. Worth adding once the per-origin cost (already dominated by one `differential_evolution` call per origin) is worth multiplying by a posterior draw count.
- [ ] Even after fixing the initial-state bug below, a smaller, genuine residual remains in the 2022-23 hiking cycle (validation region): the simulated 90% band covers the realized rate only 52-76% of the time there, worst at short maturities (see README's backtest section for the full numbers). That's the fastest, most aggressively front-loaded tightening cycle in the sample, and unlike the bug below, this looks like an actual P-measure limitation: OU `theta` is a trailing-history average with no mechanism for "policy is currently moving hard in one direction." Candidate fixes, untried: a shorter or exponentially-weighted training window so `theta` adapts faster; conditioning the simulated drift on the FOMC's own forward path (a form of the `lambda_risk`/Q-measure question above). Not patched away -- reported as found.
- [ ] Comparing against a naive random-walk baseline (`run_backtest`'s `naive_forecast` columns, added alongside the fix above) surfaced a further, separate finding: on the held-out test region, coverage is well-calibrated, but the model's *point* (median) forecast is not consistently more accurate than simply assuming the curve doesn't move -- `skill_vs_naive` is negative at most horizons and most maturities past the very short end (see README). Consistent with the well-documented difficulty of beating a random walk for interest rates, not obviously a bug -- but it means this project's honest pitch is well-calibrated *uncertainty*, not accurate point prediction, and those are different claims.
- [ ] `_wilson_interval` (the coverage confidence interval) treats each origin as an independent Bernoulli trial. Origins from overlapping windows (a 21-day step against a 63+ day horizon) aren't independent, so the reported interval is narrower than the true uncertainty. A block-bootstrap or Newey-West-style correction would be the honest fix; not implemented.
