## Stochastic simulation (`project/stochastic/`)
- [ ] `lambda_risk` (Q-measure market price of risk) is deliberately left at its zero default rather than calibrated. Two blockers, documented in `hjm_model.py::_evolve_pcs`: (1) this project only has spot Treasury yields, not derivative prices, so there's no clean data to calibrate a real price of risk against without building an empirical term-premium estimator (e.g. Fama-Bliss-style excess-return regressions); (2) it's unclear whether `lambda_risk` is even a free parameter in this construction, or redundant with the `_hjm_drift` no-arbitrage adjustment `simulate()` already applies directly to the forward curve under Q -- that relationship hasn't been derived. Needs a methodology decision before any calibration is trustworthy.
- [ ] `HJMModel.simulate_with_parameter_uncertainty` isn't wired into the walk-forward backtest (`project/stochastic/backtest.py`) -- each origin refits a plug-in point-estimate OU only. Worth adding once the per-origin cost (already dominated by one `differential_evolution` call per origin) is worth multiplying by a posterior draw count.
- [ ] The backtest (`python scripts/run_backtest.py`) surfaced a real, disclosed problem, not just plumbing: on real data, the model's simulated 90% band covers the realized rate only 4-43% of the time (nominal 90%), and it's worse, not better, in the 2022-23 hiking cycle (4-12% coverage) than in the 2024-2026 cutting cycle (7-43%) -- both were genuine, largely one-directional policy moves. The likely cause: each origin's OU `theta` (long-run mean) is estimated from trailing history, which anchors it to the *previous* rate regime, while the P-measure simulation only knows how to mean-revert toward that trailing average -- it has no mechanism for "the Fed is currently mid-cycle in one direction," which is exactly when trailing mean-reversion is the wrong model. Candidate fixes, untried: a shorter or exponentially-weighted training window so `theta` adapts faster; conditioning the simulated drift on the FOMC's own forward path (a form of the `lambda_risk`/Q-measure question above); or simply reporting this as an honest limitation of the P-measure approach rather than patching it into looking better than it is.
